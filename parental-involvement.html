Parental Involvement

Alpina treats parents as essential partners in a child’s education. Strong home-school collaboration improves learning outcomes, builds character, and supports holistic development. We emphasise open communication, mutual trust, and shared responsibility.

Parents can approach the school for information, guidance, or discussion. Individual appointments can be booked with Branch Heads, Principals, or faculty when needed. Regular Open House meetings allow confidential discussions between parents and class teachers about academic progress, personal development, and strategies for growth.

The parent’s role

  • 1. Engage and contribute

    Parents are a valued part of the Alpina community. Their interest, encouragement, and involvement help sustain our culture of excellence, discipline, and enthusiasm for learning.

  • 2. Communicate effectively

    Tell the school promptly about matters that affect your child, and keep an emergency contact number on record.

  • 3. Support learning at home

    Work with teachers to support learning and discipline, and celebrate each child’s individuality.

  • 4. Encourage a culture of learning

    Show respect for education, visit the school regularly, and read with your child every day.

Engage and contribute

Parents are welcome to take part in:

  • Co-curricular and cultural activities
  • Educational visits and field trips
  • Community service initiatives and school events

Parents with professional expertise, creative skills, or life experience are encouraged to share them. To volunteer, inform your child’s Class Teacher or Vice Principal and complete the Parent Volunteer Form, available at the School Office.

Communicate effectively

Please tell the school promptly about:

  • Medical, physical, emotional, or behavioural concerns
  • Significant changes in home circumstances
  • Updates to addresses or contact information

Support learning at home

  • Work in partnership with teachers to support learning and discipline.
  • Celebrate each child’s individuality. Avoid comparisons and focus on strengths.
  • Encourage curiosity, independence, and responsibility.
  • Foster open conversation and attentive listening at home.
  • Involve children in everyday activities and family planning.
  • Set achievable, purposeful tasks to build confidence and self-esteem.
  • Praise effort and progress rather than expecting perfection.

Encourage a culture of learning

  • Show respect for education, so your child understands that school matters.
  • Visit the school regularly, especially on Open House days.
  • Read with your child daily, even for about 20 minutes. Reading is the single most powerful learning activity.
  • Encourage writing through everyday tasks such as notes, lists, letters, and instructions.
  • Use books, digital resources, audio-visual tools, and technology to enrich learning.

Enjoy your child, celebrate growth, share in discoveries, and take pride in the journey. Education is most powerful when it is grounded in love, encouragement, and belief.
